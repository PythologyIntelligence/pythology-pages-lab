01 / Beyond pattern recognition

Correlation is where the investigation starts. Not where it ends.

Much of modern AI excels at finding statistical patterns. But three different questions must not be blurred.

CORRELATION

What changes together?

Two variables may move together because one affects the other, because they share a common cause, or because of coincidence and selection effects.

PREDICTION

What might happen next?

A system may anticipate an outcome without understanding the responsible mechanism. Accuracy alone does not establish causation.

CAUSATION

What could produce the change?

A cause requires an explicit mechanism, assumptions, evidence, alternatives and ways for the explanation to be challenged.

Pythology explores how these different forms of intelligence can work together without losing their individual scientific boundaries.

02 / The Causal Rust Core

Purpose-built. Deterministic. Inspectable.

The Causal Rust Core is an in-house computation engine developed by Pythology's founder. Written in Rust, it evaluates explicitly declared, evidence-linked causal mechanisms using directed acyclic graphs (DAGs).

Rather than treating an explanation as free-form text, we represent proposed relationships as structured components.

NODES

Represent the variables

States, conditions, decisions and outcomes become individually declared elements.

EDGES

Represent possible mechanisms

Directed relationships carry declared sign, strength, supporting evidence and confidence parameters.

INTERVENTIONS

Examine conditional alternatives

A specified variable can be changed; incoming mechanisms into that intervened node are severed in the conditional evaluation.

EXECUTION TRACES

Show the calculation

The evaluator records how effects propagate, preserving machine-readable traces and evidence references.

The engine does not assume a graph is scientifically correct. It makes the declared mechanism explicit, executable and reproducible so it can be tested.

Engine characteristics

RUST V0.2

Compiled evaluator

Strict, typed input validation, deterministic processing order, correct supported-edge propagation and bounded parameters.

PyO3 & MATURIN

Python integration

A Python-facing graph and provenance layer connected to Rust's compiled evaluation engine.

VERSIONED GRAPHS

Inspectable provenance

Graph versions, hashes, engine versions, inputs, missing information, effects and evidence references.

CONTRACT TESTING

Reproducible results

Rust-backed tests and Python/Rust integration checks support consistency across accelerated and fallback execution.

Scientific boundary: The current core propagates signed relative changes through pre-declared causal mechanisms. Its confidence propagation is a modelling heuristic, not a posterior probability of causal truth. Independent evidence is required to validate any claimed relationship.

These are active areas of AI and scientific research. Our work develops an integrated architecture around them; we do not claim to have invented causal inference, solved causal discovery or achieved universal predictive intelligence.

06 / No black-box conclusions

Make the basis and limits of every claim open to scrutiny.

OBSERVED

What did a source report?

Original evidence, timestamp and known limitations.

INFERRED

What relationships are proposed?

Assumptions, competing explanations and contradictions.

CALCULATED

What was actually executed?

Declared model, parameters, version and available trace.

PREDICTED

What was said beforehand?

Frozen expectation, confidence and evaluation window.

RESOLVED

What really happened?

Later independent evidence, misses, inconclusive results and calibration.

Not every statistical or machine-learning component is intrinsically interpretable. We distinguish opaque model contributions from deterministic causal evaluations and preserve the evidence trail around both.

Transparency isn't claiming every algorithm is simple. It's making conclusions challengeable.

07 / The scientific boundary

Advanced technology still has to answer to reality.

A deterministic calculation is not automatically a correct causal explanation. A promising precursor candidate is not a validated prediction. A successful forecast is not proof of causation. A simulated intervention is not permission for autonomous real-world action.

Confidence must be earned through suitable evidence and independent tests. The point is not to hide complexity; it is to organise it so people can question the result.
