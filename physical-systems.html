Prometheus · Physical Systems

Physical Systems

Who this is for · Engineers, infrastructure owners, manufacturers, transport operators, aerospace research teams and organisations working with instrumented equipment or structures.

Sensors can describe how a physical system is behaving. Prometheus is being developed to investigate whether changes in those measurements can reveal emerging conditions earlier, explain plausible causes and leave a traceable record for engineers to assess.

What could be studied

From a single machine to a complex structure.

Bridges & infrastructure

Strain, vibration, displacement and loading patterns could be compared against known operating conditions to guide further structural investigation.

Industrial systems

Motors, pumps, turbines and production equipment could be studied using temperature, vibration, pressure and other appropriately governed sensor streams.

Aerospace & transport

Instrumented components, test rigs and engineered assemblies could support research into changing performance, fatigue mechanisms and alternative failure hypotheses.

These are potential research applications, not claims of deployed monitoring or certified fault-detection capability in those sectors.

An evidence pathway

Measurements are the starting point.

01

Observe

Read authorised sensor data, preferably through scoped read-only interfaces.

02

Compare

Study operating baselines, variation and historical or simulated reference cases.

03

Investigate

Form alternative hypotheses and retain the measurements behind each candidate.

04

Validate

Use engineering review and prospective testing before any operational conclusion.

Where the work stands

Research foundations, not operational certification.

Pythology has explored deterministic sensor-analysis methods with simulated bearing and structural studies. The broader ambition is to connect physical observations to evidence-led Prometheus investigations. A fully integrated, deployed physical research programme has not been validated.
