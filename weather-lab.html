MOORE, OKLAHOMA · 20 MAY 2013

What the tornado replay actually found.

A historical replay used 39 KTLX Level II radar volumes recorded between 18:07 and 20:59 UTC. Inputs were gated by estimated operational availability, but the engine was developed using the same Moore case, making this an in-sample engineering replay, not independent prospective validation.

Do not claim a 26-minute warning advantage for the full detector. Radar rotation and the fused tornado hypothesis are different thresholds, and the high-confidence fused result arrived later than the official warning.

Finding revisions are the history of the same evolving investigation. Reduced radar resolution, missing scans and absent soundings changed event timing; halving azimuthal resolution proved particularly damaging. Lead time, false alarms and warning relevance still require genuinely unseen storms.

DATA SOURCES

Radar + NWS + NHC

Selected NOAA NEXRAD radar volumes, official National Weather Service alert polygons and National Hurricane Center advisory fixes support the research context. The first radar site trial was KTLX in Oklahoma.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

No global radar coverage or proprietary warning

Official meteorological advisories are not generated by Weather Lab. A missing or old feed must remain explicitly unverified.

HURRICANE MICHAEL · 2018 REPLAY

Observed and reconstructed must stay separate.

The Hurricane Michael experiment combines radar observations, NHC advisory fixes and GOES infrared context with a separately labelled seeded reconstruction of radar-unsampled inner-core regions. The reconstruction is not a validated tropical-cyclone track forecast.

RAPID INTENSIFICATION

A late result, not lead time

The first relevant revision appeared around 30 hours after a later intensification onset; the replay did not demonstrate early warning skill.

LANDFALL

An observed event already underway

The first land-interaction finding was about 30 minutes after evaluated landfall. NHC advisory positions are official context, not model-predicted tracks.

NEXT STEPS

Unseen events and robust source timing

Test held-out storm episodes, source outages, false alarms, misses, data-as-available-at-time and correct official-warning boundaries.
