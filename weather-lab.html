01 / MOORE, OKLAHOMA · 20 MAY 2013 · HISTORICAL REPLAY

The tornado experiment: what the radar test actually found.

The research engine replayed 39 KTLX Level II radar volumes recorded between 18:07 and 20:59 UTC. Its inputs were gated by estimated operational availability, so the replay did not knowingly use later official tornado reports as early evidence. The engine was also developed using this same Moore case, making it a retrospective engineering test, not an independent validation.

EARLIEST PERSISTENT RADAR ROTATION

26.4 MIN before NWS warning

First radar precursor

First persistent rotation at 19:13:35 UTC, about 42.4 minutes before touchdown and 26.4 minutes before the first NWS tornado warning at 19:40 UTC.

This early classification had 0.64 confidence, not a validated tornado probability; the same event was used while building and tuning the analysis engine.

HIGH-CONFIDENCE ROTATION

9.3 MIN before NWS warning

Rotation intensified in the replay

The first high-confidence rotation classification was emitted at 19:30:41 UTC, 9.3 minutes before the warning and 25.3 minutes before touchdown, with confidence 0.72.

Recognising rotating storm structure does not mean accurately forecasting tornado formation or issuing a public warning.

HIGH-CONFIDENCE FUSED STORM STRUCTURE

7.8 MIN after NWS warning

The finding that came too late

The fused tornado-supportive-structure hypothesis did not reach 0.7 confidence until 19:47:47 UTC — 7.8 minutes after the NWS warning and 8.2 minutes before touchdown.

That negative result prevents us claiming the full detector demonstrated a 26-minute warning advantage.

REPRODUCIBILITY · ENGINE RECORD

168 finding revisions

Not 168 separate predictions

The pinned Moore baseline processed 68 engine inputs and recorded 168 finding revisions. Revisions preserve how evolving storm evidence changed classifications over time.

Missing scans, reduced radar resolution and absent soundings produced different timings; halved azimuthal resolution was particularly damaging.

Scientific boundary: One tornado, one development case and one radar do not establish forecast skill, a population false-alarm rate or a reliable warning lead-time distribution. Results are retrospective; NWS warning decisions are not equivalent to model finding timestamps.

03 / HURRICANE MICHAEL · OCTOBER 2018 · HISTORICAL REPLAY

A 3D tropical-cyclone reconstruction — not a validated track forecast.

Observed and reconstructed, clearly separated

The 2018 Hurricane Michael experiment combines observed radar, advisory fixes and GOES infrared context with a separately labelled, seeded model reconstruction for radar-unsampled inner-core regions. The interface distinguishes observed/derived layers from reconstructed estimates and their uncertainty.

Negative results remain in the record

The rapid-intensification precursor did not offer demonstrated lead over the truth onset in the evaluated replay; the first relevant revision appeared around 30 hours after a later intensification onset. The first land-interaction finding came 30 minutes after evaluated landfall. Live NHC advisory fixes are context only and do not yet drive a live tropical prediction engine.

04 / NEXT TESTS · SCIENTIFIC AND OPERATIONAL

What we need to prove next.

Prospective radar evaluation

Collect genuinely unseen storm episodes. Predefine target events and matching criteria, then measure false alarms, misses, robustness, source gaps and reproducible availability-time lead.

Geographical expansion

Add NEXRAD stations and independent regional weather agencies only where source provenance and operational access are verified. Western-Pacific typhoons require separate JMA/JTWC/RSMC integrations.

Separate hypotheses from triggers

Investigate any solar-weather statistical relationship with quiet-weather controls, confounder checks and pre-registered analysis. Do not use solar observations to raise weather hazard scores in the current system.

Weather Lab is not a warning service. For actual protective action, always follow the responsible national meteorological service (including the U.S. National Weather Service and National Hurricane Center). Radar-derived model confidence is not a calibrated tornado probability.
