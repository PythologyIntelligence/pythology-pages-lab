Computational Biology · Molecular Intelligence · Physical Intelligence

How do biological processes change before disease becomes apparent? How do molecular structures influence biological function? How can intelligent systems understand the behaviour of machines, materials and connected physical infrastructure?

We're developing computational research systems combining artificial intelligence, mathematical modelling, structured scientific evidence and causal reasoning.

The objective isn't simply to recognise patterns. It's to understand what those patterns might mean — and discover how to test that understanding.

01 / Computational Biology

Investigating the signals within living systems.

Living systems operate across interconnected scales. Changes in genes influence proteins, proteins participate in pathways, and pathways shape cellular behaviour, tissue function and the health of organisms.

Our research explores connecting biological observations, evaluating mechanisms and identifying hypotheses worthy of further investigation.

BIO-SYMBOLOGY

Connecting biological patterns with structured scientific reasoning.

This research direction combines computational pattern analysis with explicit representations of genes, pathways, cellular processes, disease mechanisms and scientific evidence. Contradictions and uncertainty remain visible.

MULTI-OMIC BIOREACTOR ENGINE

From molecular observations to testable hypotheses.

The six-stage prototype investigates genomic variants, cellular states, tissue contexts, intervention hypotheses, evidence dossiers, cohort patterns and experimental feedback. Model interpretations are kept separate from observed biological facts.

PROTEUS MOLECULAR INTELLIGENCE

Exploring molecular structure and function.

Proteus is a research workbench for protein-sequence exploration, variants, structural context, controlled molecular design hypotheses and evidence tracking. Current foundation demonstrations are reproducible calculations, not validated proteins or therapeutics.

BIO-SYMBOLIC TRANSCRIPTOMIC SCREENING

Investigating compound–disease signature relationships.

A Rust-accelerated statistical engine explores compounds whose measured gene-expression signatures computationally oppose disease-associated expression patterns. These are research candidates, not evidence of therapeutic safety or efficacy.

SYMBIOSCREEN

Can blood gene-expression patterns reveal biological abnormalities?

SymbioScreen researches a 500-gene blood-transcriptomic panel using healthy-reference anomaly detection, calibration, quality checks and separated synthetic evaluation. An abnormality score is not a disease probability; clinical diagnostic performance remains unvalidated.

What connects the biological research?

Although these projects address different biological problems, they share a philosophy: connect observations across scales, preserve evidence provenance, keep hypotheses distinct from results, record contradictions and incorporate independently measured feedback.

Evidence → hypothesis → investigation → experimental evidence → revision.
Research boundary: the systems are at different development stages, and their integration remains a research objective. None offers clinical diagnosis, treatment recommendations or experimentally validated therapies.

02 / Physical Intelligence

Intelligence that respects the laws of physics.

Engineering systems operate under constraints: energy, force, temperature, geometry, materials, capacity, time and uncertainty.

An AI system may sound convincing, but if its explanation violates the governing physics it is not useful intelligence.

TECHNOSPHERE TWIN

Understanding how disruption moves through infrastructure.

This recovered research prototype demonstrates controlled scenarios for energy networks, ports, freight corridors and supply chains, using synthetic fixtures, explicit assumptions and human-reviewed alternatives. It cannot operate real-world infrastructure.

PHYSICALLY CONSTRAINED INTELLIGENCE

Building models that answer to the real world.

Our broader programme investigates mathematical models, computational reasoning and physical constraints for defined engineering problems. Potential research areas include industrial equipment health, aerospace durability, advanced manufacturing, materials and resilience.

The objective is not one universal simulator but focused, testable models constrained by physical laws and measured evidence.

03 / One Research Philosophy

Different scientific domains. The same demand for evidence.

OBSERVE

What was actually measured?

Preserve source, provenance and limitations.

MODEL

What might connect it?

Represent mechanisms and relationships explicitly.

QUESTION

What else could explain the result?

Keep alternatives, missing information and contradictions.

TEST

What does independent evidence show?

Compare expectations with suitable experiments or measurements.

LEARN

How should our understanding change?

Update the research record without rewriting the original evidence.

Methods can be shared across disciplines, but validation, confidence and scientific expertise must be earned separately in each domain.

04 / Research, Not Unverified Promises

Scientific progress requires more than impressive demonstrations.

Our computational biology and physical-intelligence systems are active research projects at different stages of maturity. Some demonstrate working engines and reproducible calculations; others are prototypes, controlled simulations or developing architectures.

None should be confused with completed clinical validation, certified engineering performance or proven scientific discovery.

The measure of progress is not how confidently a system answers. It is how well its answers survive independent testing.

From molecular processes governing life to the physical systems supporting modern civilisation, we are exploring how intelligence can contribute to genuine understanding.
