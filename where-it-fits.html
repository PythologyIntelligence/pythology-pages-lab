Where It Fits

Where earlier awareness
could make a difference.

Environmental change affects almost every part of our lives. From the growing risks faced by insurers to the challenges confronting conservation teams, emergency services and scientific researchers, the need to understand what is changing is universal.

At Pythology Intelligence, we're exploring how environmental monitoring, precursor research and accountable AI can help organisations recognise significant changes, investigate emerging risks and make better-informed decisions.

Different challenges. Different environments. The same ambition: better information, sooner.

Who could benefit?

Research with real-world applications.

01 / INSURANCE & REINSURANCE

Understanding risk before the full impact is known.

Insurers and reinsurers face exposure to severe weather, flooding, volcanic activity and other hazards. Our research could complement catastrophe models with evolving environmental observations and geographical context — not replace actuarial, underwriting or claims expertise.

02 / GOVERNMENT & CONSERVATION

Protecting communities and the natural world.

Government departments, councils and conservation agencies monitor complex environments. Chathams EcoWatch, developed for the Department of Conservation (DOC), demonstrates how focused ecological monitoring and baselines can help specialists identify meaningful change.

03 / WEATHER & NATURAL HAZARD RESEARCH

Investigating what happens before the event.

Meteorological and geophysical researchers study severe storms, seismic activity and volcanic unrest. Prometheus tests possible precursor patterns against later events and non-event comparisons to learn which signals, if any, provide reliable advance information.

04 / EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT & INFRASTRUCTURE

More time to understand developing conditions.

Natural hazards can put power, transport, communications and water under pressure simultaneously. Environmental evidence could help specialists understand emerging risks — not replace official warnings, safety procedures or engineering judgement.

05 / ENVIRONMENTAL & MARINE MONITORING

Recognising changes that happen quietly.

Changes in vegetation, ecosystems, coastal waters and marine environments may develop over months or years. Environmental monitoring establishes baselines and identifies anomalies worth investigating.

06 / SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH & ACADEMIA

Finding questions worth investigating.

Potential collaboration spans geophysics, weather research, causal models, precursor-signal testing and probabilistic forecasting. Our goal is to investigate ideas rigorously, including negative and inconclusive results.

PHYSICAL INTELLIGENCE · LIVE MONITORING RESEARCH & PILOTS

Have sensors?
Let's make sense of what changes.

For a business, researcher, asset owner or small operator: Physical Intelligence could connect to an authorised live sensor feed or existing equipment data, track behaviour over time, compare meaningful changes with baselines and give your team a private place to inspect the evidence. You don't need to own a giant industrial plant to start a conversation.

01 / BUSINESS & INDUSTRY

Equipment you depend on

Factories, workshops, building operators and maintenance teams: pumps, motors, HVAC, compressors, refrigeration or production machinery. Examine temperature, vibration, load, energy and performance changes against their own normal patterns.

02 / ASSETS & ESSENTIAL SERVICES

Buildings and infrastructure

Water networks, reservoirs, bridges, energy systems and facilities: investigate authorised pressure, flow, strain, movement or operating data, with engineering expertise and appropriate safety boundaries.

03 / GROWING & RESEARCH

From greenhouses to laboratories

Growers, environmental teams, universities and individual equipment owners: investigate irrigation, climate controls, environmental stations or experimental apparatus wherever suitable measurement data is available.

USING THE SENSORS YOU ALREADY HAVE

Sensor or gateway → approved API → Physical Intelligence → private dashboard

With compatible, permissioned data, the Rust research engine can receive approved HTTP, MQTT or WebSocket feeds—or mapped historical records. A proposed pilot would examine data quality, record a baseline, screen changes against the system's real operating modes and present traceable findings in a private dashboard. The connection and analysis depend on permissions, suitable sensor coverage, sampling, calibration, security and domain-specific models.

This capability is under active research. Our validated public examples currently use synthetic machinery and idealised structural calculations; no live customer asset is represented as being monitored.
