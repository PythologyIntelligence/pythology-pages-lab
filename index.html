A forecast is not a decision.

Prometheus tells you what might happen. Decision Futures asks what changes if an authorised human chooses differently — while keeping the baseline, assumptions and uncertainty visible.

One chain. Different jobs.

00 EarthNet

What is happening? Multi-source ingestion — atmosphere, ocean, seismic, volcanic, humanitarian, infrastructure, space weather, near-earth objects.

01 Evidence & State

What do we actually know? Observation stays separate from inference. Claims stay separate from facts. The evidence record is clean before anything reasons about it.

02 Causal layer

What might connect it? Link signals only through explicit mechanisms. Preserve competing explanations. "These two things moved together" is not enough.

03 Prometheus

What should happen next? Timestamped, uncertainty-weighted forecasts. Immutable forecast IDs. Public resolution when the horizon closes. Every miss stays in the ledger.

04 Decision Futures

What changes if the choice changes? Compare baseline vs. intervention vs. alternative. Track what happened on the path you chose — and the path you didn't.

05 Sentinel

Can a human challenge the whole thing? "Why do you believe this?" "What would change your mind?" The system answers, or it doesn't earn trust.

Outcome

Were we right? The forecast resolves. The score updates. The calibration improves — or it doesn't. Either way, the evidence is public.

Who this is for

Insurance & reinsurance

When a severe-weather system is forming, the question is not only what happened, but which exposures are changing before the claims data exists.

Government & emergency management

Important signals often appear as fragments across different systems. The need is for one challengeable operating picture early enough to support better decisions.

Critical infrastructure & utilities

Weather, seismicity and downstream dependencies can all shift around an asset at once. The question becomes what deserves attention first, and why.

Research & strategic intelligence

If a hypothesis is worth taking seriously, it should be able to say what should happen next and be judged later against reality.

Why this exists

It started with one stubborn question: what is actually causing this? Not what correlates. Not what the model predicts. What is the mechanism — and how would we know if we were wrong?

Pythology was built on weekends over six months by one person with a day job. No team. No funding. Just the conviction that intelligence systems should be able to explain themselves, forecast honestly, and keep the record when they're wrong.

Brent Robertson · Manawatū, New Zealand
