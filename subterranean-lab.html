NEW ZEALAND · HISTORICAL REPLAYS

Earthquakes selected after the outcome.

Known historical events were used to evaluate geological screening rules. A multi-signal spatial cell is not an independently confirmed predicted earthquake.

Historical eventMulti-signal spatial cellsWhat the evaluation revealed
Christchurch · 22 Feb 20115 of 33The early classification survived inclusion of revised GeoNet data, but changed in geographic extent and duration.
Kaikōura · 14 Nov 20163 of 31The previously reported near-epicentral signal did not survive the updated catalogue.
Fiordland · 15 Jul 20094 of 33The earliest regional classification was distant from the eventual mainshock.
Historical catalogues may contain revisions unavailable at the time. Multiple checkpoints in one spatial cell are correlated observations, not separate forecasts. These studies do not establish genuine prospective lead time, false-alarm rates or prediction accuracy.
ERUPTION CONTEXT · SELECTED CASES

Volcanic settings still require their own observations.

The initial eruption-selected analyses screened seismic catalogues around regions known to have erupted. They did not include a validated continuous network of volcanic gas, deformation, thermal or tremor observations.

Eruption caseMulti-signal cellsImportant qualification
Nabro · 12–13 Jun 20114 of 49The central cell did not qualify. The first reported regional multi-signal classification was approximately 2.5 hours before a literature-inferred onset, not a prospective prediction.
Whakaari · 9 Dec 201925 of 49The central cell did not qualify; many classifications arose across the wider Bay of Plenty.
La Palma · 19 Sep 20219 of 49The overlapping cells were not independent. The earliest regional classification was around seven days before eruption in an event-selected replay.
